Safety, Accessibility & Nondiscrimination Policy

Seacoast RVIP LLC  ·  Effective October 31, 2026

Issued by Seacoast RVIP LLC, a New Hampshire limited liability company based in Hampton, NH. See all legal documents.

Seacoast RVIP LLC requires safe, lawful and nondiscriminatory transportation in its own operation and from participating partner carriers. This policy applies to drivers, managers, carrier representatives and riders. Partner carriers remain responsible for their personnel and transportation; RVIP remains responsible for its own conduct and platform controls.

Section 1
Immediate safety

Call 911 for an emergency. Do not use an app message as a substitute for emergency assistance. A driver must stop or refuse unsafe operation, move to a safe place when possible and promptly notify the carrier and RVIP. Report a serious incident as soon as safe and no later than 24 hours after learning of it, without delaying a legally required earlier report. Preserve relevant evidence and cooperate with lawful investigations.

Section 2
Fitness for duty

Driving while impaired by alcohol, illegal drugs, misused medication or another condition that prevents safe driving is prohibited. Drivers must not use a handheld device in violation of law, drive while dangerously fatigued, or continue when a medical condition makes driving unsafe. Applicable hours-of-service rules and the carrier's fatigue controls apply. No booking, payment or management instruction overrides the duty to stop unsafe operation.

RVIP may immediately restrict access during a credible safety investigation. It evaluates the evidence, applicable employment obligations and opportunities to respond before determining continuing eligibility. A test override cannot override a safety restriction for a live trip.

Section 3
Driver and vehicle eligibility

Drivers must hold the appropriate current license, satisfy any screening their carrier is required to perform, and satisfy applicable carrier and state requirements. Personal onboarding includes both sides of the license and issue and expiration dates where present. Vehicle onboarding identifies the actual vehicle and records registration, commercial insurance, inspections and plate evidence applicable to that vehicle and service. Drivers may operate only an approved, insured vehicle for the assigned carrier.

An insurance card or certificate does not by itself establish all coverage terms. The policy and relevant endorsements must support the actual vehicle, driver, territory and for-hire use. A single verified insurance packet may establish several requirements. Requirements that do not legally apply must be recorded as not applicable with supporting reasoning, rather than falsely marked as satisfied.

Section 4
Equal access

Discrimination, harassment and retaliation based on a legally protected characteristic are prohibited. Drivers must provide lawful service without discriminatory cancellation, pricing or treatment. Partner discretion to decline an offer is not permission to discriminate. Threats, abusive behavior and unwanted sexual conduct are prohibited for all users.

Section 5
Service animals

Qualified service animals must be accommodated as required by law without an extra fee. Do not demand a certificate, identification card or demonstration. Where permitted and the need is not apparent, staff may ask whether the animal is required because of a disability and what work or task it is trained to perform. Any exclusion must meet the applicable legal standard and be based on the actual circumstances, not a stereotype or another passenger's preference. Continue to offer lawful transportation to the person when an animal is properly excluded.

Section 6
Mobility and accessible transportation

Drivers must reasonably assist with permitted mobility aids and must not impose a disability surcharge. Assess safe securement, vehicle capacity and the person's actual needs without assumptions. RVIP must determine and meet the accessibility and equivalent-service obligations applicable to its service and vehicle classes under the ADA and transportation regulations. A sport utility vehicle must not automatically be treated as an automobile for an exemption, and a referral alone is not proof that an equivalent-service obligation has been met.

When the assigned vehicle cannot lawfully or safely meet a requested accessibility need, notify management promptly to arrange an appropriate lawful solution. Do not promise an accessible vehicle that is unavailable or misrepresent the scope of the fleet. Support and complaint channels must provide reasonable communication accommodations.

Section 7
Riders and trip conduct

The current service is limited to adults aged 18 or older; minors are not transported, including when accompanied. This is a company eligibility policy, not a statement that every law prohibits transportation of minors. Occupants must use required restraints. Do not exceed the manufacturer's rated seating capacity. Illegal activity, violence and conduct creating a material safety risk are prohibited.

The driver must verify the correct reservation and pickup. Riders should match the displayed provider, vehicle and plate. The trip must not start until payment is server-confirmed. Drivers may not pressure a rider to use an unauthorized payment method or make an undisclosed charge.

Section 8
Privacy and evidence

RVIP does not authorize cabin audio or video recording under this policy. Any future recording program requires a separate legal and privacy process before use. Protect rider and driver information, limit communications to legitimate purposes and do not post trip details publicly. Do not destroy incident records subject to an investigation or legal hold.

Section 9
Complaints and corrective action

Report safety, accessibility or discrimination concerns through in-app support or info@seacoastrvip.com. Provide the reservation identifier and relevant facts without exposing unnecessary sensitive information. RVIP records the complaint, assesses immediate protection needs, investigates impartially and communicates the outcome to the extent permitted by privacy and employment law. No person may retaliate for a good-faith report or participation in an investigation.

Corrective action may include training, a vehicle restriction, suspension, termination of platform access, employment action or a report required by law. Keep the action proportionate to the findings and distinguish allegations from substantiated facts. Statutory reporting and appeal rights remain available.
